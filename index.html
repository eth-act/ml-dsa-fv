Before and after

Everything that got faster, on one log scale. Grey lines are files nobody touched: they stayed put, which is how you know the rest is real.

02

Where the time went

Every proof walks an EVM state through hundreds of bytecode instructions. Lean's kernel then re-checks the result. Three quarters of the CPU sat in those symbolic-execution proofs, and the kernel's cost grew faster than the block length.

03

How: measure, profile, fix the shared tactic, gate

Nobody edited proofs one at a time to chase seconds. Each round timed every module alone, profiled the slowest, named the cause, and fixed it once in shared tactics or proof generators. All three parameter sets inherited each fix.

08

Proof that nothing changed

A faster proof is worthless if it proves something weaker. Every merge passed a gate that compares every constant's statement and axioms, replays every module in Lean's kernel, and regenerates every artifact. A final run compared the starting commit with master from scratch.

09

Then the bet changed: gas

The maintainer meant the verifier, not the build: 2× less gas to check a signature on-chain. We kept every rule fixed: the EIP-8355 interface, compressed public keys, real SHAKE, no helper contracts, and the same verdict on every test vector. Then we went after the gas.
